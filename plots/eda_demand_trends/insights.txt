ACN data models workplace/campus charging behavior.
These peak hours and congestion patterns differ significantly from public city (CBD) charging.

UrbanEV Insights (CBD vs Non-CBD):
1. Scale Differences: Average demand per pile in CBD areas is surprisingly low, fluctuating between 2-4 kWh per day. In contrast, Non-CBD (workplace/residential) areas see much higher per-pile utilization, fluctuating between 5-10 kWh per day. This proves that while CBD grids as a whole handle more total energy, they are heavily over-provisioned with chargers, leading to lower utilization per individual pile compared to Non-CBD areas.
2. Volatility: The CBD graph seems to have sharper, more dramatic relative drops (perhaps on specific weekends or holidays?), whereas the non-CBD areas show a slightly more stable oscillation pattern due to consistent commuting/residential charging.

Pricing Implications & Prediction Agent Insights:
1. Demand Predictability (Prediction Agent): Workplace/Residential charging (Non-CBD) exhibits massive, highly predictable patterns based on the workweek. The Prediction Agent must heavily weight 'day_of_week' and 'is_weekend'.
2. Price Elasticity & User Behavior: 
   - CBD areas are Highly ELASTIC. Because they are over-provisioned with chargers (low utilization per pile), customers have endless alternatives. If prices surge, they will easily drive to the cheaper station next door.
   - Non-CBD areas are Highly INELASTIC. Because they have massive commuter peaks and high utilization per pile, customers are locked-in during morning rushes. They have no alternatives and must pay the surge price.
3. Surge Pricing vs. Discounting (Pricing Agent Objectives): 
   - CBD Pricing Agent Objective: Focus heavily on "Off-Peak Discounts" to aggressively steal price-sensitive customers from neighboring grids and fill idle piles.
   - Workplace Pricing Agent Objective: Focus heavily on "Surge Pricing" during peak commuter hours to maximize revenue from trapped, price-insensitive commuters, forcing load-shifting to reduce wait times.

Architecture Implications (Agent Design):
- Context-Aware Agents: Instead of a one-size-fits-all model, the system should use the CBD status to shift its optimization metrics.

Data Strategy (Prediction Model):
- Model Training Data: The Demand Prediction Model should be trained exclusively on the UrbanEV (Shenzhen) dataset.
- Justification: Cross-verification of the ACN (US Workplace) and UrbanEV Non-CBD (Shenzhen Workplace/Residential) plots reveals nearly identical structural trends (massive weekend drop-offs, consistent commuting peaks). Therefore, the UrbanEV dataset acts as a comprehensive "superset." By using it exclusively, we avoid geographic domain-shift issues while still allowing the model to learn both distinct charging behaviors (Workplace vs Public) simply by relying on the 'CBD' feature flag.
- Generalization via Spatial Attributes (Option 3): The `grid` ID must be excluded from the feature list entirely during training. Instead, the model relies solely on spatial and geographical physics: `lon`, `la`, `CBD`, `area`, `grid_adj_count` (Network connectivity), and `grid_mean_dist` (Proximity).
  * Benefit: This approach prevents overfitting to specific grid numbers and allows the model to predict demand for a newly built charging station anywhere in the world simply by inputting its physical location and connectivity features.

4. Grid-Specific Surging Thresholds (Pricing Agent Optimization):
- The 30-day time-series plot of all 275 grids (`all_grids_30_day_trend.png`) exposes extreme variance in structural utilization rates between grids. Some grids naturally peak around 10% daily average utilization, while others regularly exceed 50%.
- The Pricing Agent must calculate dynamic, grid-specific thresholds. For instance, surging when a grid's current utilization exceeds the 90th percentile of its own historical distribution. This guarantees maximum revenue by reacting accurately to the local physics and demand ceiling of each individual 1km square.

5. Universal Network Load Balancing Thresholds:
- To enable true "Network Balancing" (where the AI physically moves cars between adjacent zones using prices as a magnet), thresholds cannot be entirely grid-specific. They must be grouped.
- By splitting the network into CBD and Non-CBD groups, we establish objective, network-wide baseline thresholds:
  * CBD Thresholds: Surge > 29.38% (80th Pct), Discount < 8.66% (20th Pct)
  * Non-CBD Thresholds: Surge > 34.94% (80th Pct), Discount < 9.80% (20th Pct)
- If a CBD station hits 30% utilization, it surges. If a nearby Non-CBD station is at 30% utilization, it remains at normal price (since it is below its 34.94% threshold). This price delta acts as a financial magnet, pulling excess demand out of the CBD and into the Non-CBD zone!
